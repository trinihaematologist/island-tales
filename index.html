Stories travel

One sea, many storytellers

Caribbean folklore grew from Indigenous traditions and from stories carried by African, European, Indian and other communities. Oral storytelling reshaped them over generations.

Tap a marker

Folklore changes as it travels.

A name, appearance or lesson may shift from one island to another. That variation is part of the tradition.

Why these stories matter

Folklore is more than something spooky.

These stories helped communities explain danger, nature, behaviour and belonging. A forest guardian could remind people to respect animals. A river spirit could warn against polluting water. A trickster could show that wit sometimes beats strength.

For grown-ups & teachers

Use the stories as a starting point. Ask children whether their family knows a different version, what lesson they see in it, and why the story might have survived.

Important: Caribbean Island Tales uses child-friendly retellings. Traditional versions can be darker and vary significantly by storyteller and community.
